# Great and weak buying experiences

## Great (and the feeling they nail)

**A Pilates studio (Simon's own story).** Simon wanted a weekly high-intensity Pilates class. Two studios were cheap, one was very expensive: better teachers, better playlists, a nicer room, better ventilation. He picked the expensive one, and right after paying he felt a few seconds of guilt (buyer's remorse). Then a WhatsApp message arrived from the studio, welcoming him to their community. It created connection, and it confirmed his decision to pay up for quality. Neither cheap studio sent one. Oxytocin + serotonin, from one short message.

**The Apple Store.** You hold the product before anyone mentions a price. Apple's staff training, reported by the Wall Street Journal in 2011, listed the steps of service: approach, probe to understand needs, present, listen, end with a warm goodbye. Probing comes before presenting. Dopamine first, price later.

**The Ritz-Carlton.** Employees may spend up to $2,000 to fix a guest's problem without asking a manager. The guest gets the fix and the feeling someone is on their side. Oxytocin.

**Zappos.** A support rep once stayed on one call for more than ten hours. People still tell the story; few remember what was ordered.

**Harley-Davidson.** The purchase starts the story: owners' group rides, rallies, miles together. Endorphins + oxytocin.

## Weak (and what they kill)

**The classic car dealership.** Price first, haggling, "let me check with my manager". The pain of paying with no reward to balance it. Saturn built a brand in the 1990s on removing the haggle.

**The demo-first sales call** (made up, very common). 45 minutes of slides, the price on the last slide. The buyer never said what's broken. No dopamine, no ownership. Then ghosting.

**"Nice bookshelf behind you."** Small talk dressed up as rapport. Safe, creates nothing.

**The proposal that went out too early.** The buyer never said what doing nothing costs, so the price sits alone on the page looking big. The thread goes quiet.

## The B2B translation
The person signing your contract has the same brain as the person holding the iPhone. Strategy Sprints sends every new client a personal welcome message from Simon on day one, for exactly the reason the Pilates studio did.
